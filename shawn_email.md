The issue is US Fish and Wildlife Service wetland easements in North Dakota's prairie pothole region. Much of the concern is about the setback requirements around easement wetlands and what they cost producers. It came up in both of our meetings with Senator Hoeven's staff yesterday, and it's getting a lot of attention right now. Senator Cramer has had a blanket hold on Senate nominees for the last couple of months until FWS fixes the easement problem. Last week Hoeven, Cramer and Rep. Fedorchak held a roundtable at Interior with producers and wildlife groups.

The data are thin. At that roundtable Steve Hansen put the area governed by the setbacks at about 4.5 million acres. It has also been quoted as 4.2 million. Hoeven's staff aren't sure the number is right, and one of them suspects it was extrapolated from a handful of operators. They'd like us to test it.

There's also a policy angle. FWS easement wetlands can't be mitigated the way NRCS wetlands can, and FWS has been running a pilot that works a bit like a mitigation option. As the staffer described it (he said it was a big oversimplification), a producer can take mapped easement wetlands under an acre and move them to an unmapped spot, combining them into one larger wetland. The new wetland stays under FWS setback rules. FWS wants to make the pilot a permanent program for ND. The farm groups have been talking about this kind of consolidation as a fix, but nobody has looked at whether the economics work.

Here's what we’d like you to look into:

1. Acreage. How many acres are actually affected? First figure out what the 4.2 to 4.5 million is supposed to count. It could be total acres in easement tracts, wetland acres inside them, or cropland actually constrained by setbacks, and those could be very different numbers. Then see what we can build from GIS data. It would also be worth checking how many of the mapped basins have held water in recent years.

2. Cost to the producer. A rough per-acre estimate of what the setbacks cost: extra operating cost from farming around basins and buffers, plus lost revenue on ground that can't be cropped.

3. Consolidation. A simple framework for the net effect of the pilot, i.e. the acres and field efficiency gained by removing small basins, minus what the new wetland and its setback take. We'll need the actual pilot terms from FWS, so for now a list of what we'd need from them is fine.

If you can start by pulling together for us short internal memo would be great: where the data are, a first stab at the acreage number, and rough cost ranges. If some piece of this isn't realistic in a week, let me know early and we'll adjust.
